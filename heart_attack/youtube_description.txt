Having a heart attack? Do these 4 things immediately, because every minute you wait costs heart muscle you can't get back.

In this video, Dr. Eliahs Robert, a physician with 30 years of practice, explains exactly what to do in the first minutes of a heart attack, step by step and backed by research. You'll learn the heart attack warning signs that don't involve chest pain (especially in women and adults over 60), why you should call 911 instead of driving to the hospital, how to chew aspirin the right way and who should NOT take it, when to use your prescribed nitroglycerin, the simple step that gets paramedics to you faster, and how hands-only CPR and an AED can save someone who collapses. We also bust the "cough CPR" myth and reveal the one mistake almost everyone makes before they even pick up the phone.

The 4 steps: CALL 911 → CHEW aspirin (only if it's safe for you) → SIT down → UNLOCK the door.

⚠️ MEDICAL DISCLAIMER
This video is for educational purposes only and is not medical advice. It does not replace emergency services or your own doctor. If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack, call 911 (or your local emergency number) immediately. Do not take aspirin if you are allergic to it, have active or recent bleeding, or have a bleeding disorder. If you take blood thinners, follow the dispatcher's instructions. Never give aspirin for stroke symptoms. Only use nitroglycerin prescribed to you, and never after erectile-dysfunction medication. Ask your doctor for a personal heart attack action plan.
