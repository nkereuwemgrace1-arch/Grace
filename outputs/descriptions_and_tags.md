## VIDEO 1 — The UNTOLD Story and History of Rolex in 11 Minutes

### Description

Rolex makes around a million watches a year, yet some models still have waitlists. It has no shareholders, no stock price and no dividends, because it's owned by a charity. And it was started by a 24-year-old orphan selling a product men refused to wear.

This is the untold story of Rolex: from Hans Wilsdorf's tiny London company in 1905 to the most recognized luxury watch brand on Earth. You'll find out how a "bracelet for ladies" became a symbol of success, why a swimmer who never finished the English Channel became watch history's first brand ambassador, how Rolex mailed watches into WWII prison camps on trust, how it survived the quartz crisis that wiped out hundreds of Swiss brands, and why a Daytona that once collected dust sold for about $17.8 million.

In this video:
• Hans Wilsdorf: orphan, pearl clerk, and the man who bet everything on wristwatches
• The mystery behind the name "Rolex"
• The Oyster: the first truly waterproof wristwatch, and the Channel swim that sold it
• Watches behind barbed wire: Rolex and the prisoners of WWII
• The Hans Wilsdorf Foundation: why nobody can buy Rolex
• Everest, the Mariana Trench and the Submariner, GMT-Master and Day-Date
• The quartz crisis: how Rolex went up when everyone else went down
• Waitlists, resale prices, Bucherer and Rolex today

## VIDEO 2 — The UNTOLD Story of Coca-Cola and Its Pathway to the Market in 12 Minutes

### Description

You can grab an ice-cold Coca-Cola on a beach in Brazil, in a mountain town in Nepal, or at a tiny kiosk in a Kenyan village. How does one drink from Atlanta end up in almost every corner of the planet?

The secret recipe isn't the real story. The pathway to market is. This is the untold story of how Coca-Cola built the most powerful distribution machine in business history: from a pharmacy selling about nine glasses a day in 1886, to free-sample coupons handed out across America, a bottling deal signed for just $1, a bottle designed to be recognized in the dark, a price frozen at 5 cents for about 70 years, a world war that carried Coke across the oceans, and local entrepreneurs delivering crates by hand cart to the last mile.

In this video:
• John Pemberton and the 1886 soda counter with no road to market
• Asa Candler's free-coupon machine and the "sell syrup, not drinks" idea
• The $1 bottling deal, and the "forever" contract that came back to bite
• The contour bottle, inspired by the wrong plant
• Robert Woodruff's "arm's reach of desire": cartons, coolers and vending machines
• The 5-cent price that lasted around 70 years
• WWII: how Coke went global, and how Fanta was born
• Anchor bottlers, refranchising and the "own the brand, let partners own the trucks" model
• The Last Mile: Manual Distribution Centers in East Africa
• Share a Coke, and the challenges Coca-Cola faces today
